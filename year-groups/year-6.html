Autumn 1 Big Question

How can learning from history help us challenge injustice?

Learning presentationOpen Afternoon
Explore this unit +
The learning journey

Year 6 begin with a challenging enquiry into injustice and the responsibility people have to confront it. Their study of Benin develops historical understanding while also raising questions about power, representation and the Benin Bronzes. In science, pupils investigate the circulatory system and healthy lifestyles, supported by Centre of the Cell’s ‘In a HeartBeat’ workshop. Persuasive speeches, reporting and art linked to activism give pupils ways to use their own voices, placing Integrity at the centre of the learning.

Learning presentationOpen AfternoonAuthentic audience: Families
Trip / visit / workshop‘In a HeartBeat’ workshop – Centre of the Cell

Writing outcomes

Pupil Parliament persuasive speeches; informal letter from Papa Eze to Ada; newspaper report

Curriculum focus

History: Benin · Science: circulatory system and healthy lifestyles · Art: printmaking, activism and Benin Bronzes

Autumn 2 Big Question

What makes people feel they belong?

Learning presentationKS2 Performance
Explore this unit +
The learning journey

Through the story of the Windrush Generation, pupils explore migration, identity and the powerful human need to belong. Notting Hill Carnival provides a cultural starting point before geography examines the Caribbean, Fairtrade and migration. Science develops understanding of evolution and inheritance, while writing draws on journeys, voices and place. Camden’s Citizenship Scheme extends the theme into pupils’ own lives, helping them think about Respect, community and their growing responsibilities as young citizens.

KCA ValueRespect
Initial experienceWindrush Generation – Notting Hill Carnival
Learning presentationKS2 PerformanceAuthentic audience: Families
Trip / visit / workshopCamden Citizenship Scheme – The Salvation Army, Chalk Farm

Writing outcomes

Alliteration poetry; Windrush journey diary; information writing about the Caribbean

Curriculum focus

Geography: Fairtrade in the Caribbean and migration · Science: evolution and inheritance · Art: drawing and sketchbooks

Spring 1 Big Question

What can we learn from the resilience of those in World War 1?

Learning presentationWhole School Exhibition on The Street
Explore this unit +
The learning journey

World War 1 becomes the context for an enquiry into resilience, sacrifice and the experiences of ordinary people. An immersive WW1 Day establishes the period before pupils investigate historical evidence and the realities of trench life. Science explores living things and habitats, while design technology connects to the past through wartime cooking. Letters, narratives and speeches encourage pupils to consider different perspectives and communicate with purpose, returning throughout to the value of Endurance.

Learning presentationWhole School Exhibition on The StreetAuthentic audience: KCA Community
Trip / visit / workshopBritish Library – Windrush Voices

Writing outcomes

Formal letter to the King and Archbishop of Canterbury; trench-life flashback narrative; speech: ‘Children Should Never Be Enemies’

Curriculum focus

History: World War 1 · Science: living things and habitats · DT: WW1 cooking

Spring 2 Big Question

How can ordinary people make an extraordinary difference?

Learning presentationWhole School Exhibition on The Street
Explore this unit +
The learning journey

Year 6 move into World War 2 through the experience of evacuation, asking how ordinary people can make an extraordinary difference. Evacuation Day gives pupils an immediate human perspective before historical enquiry examines life during wartime. The Imperial War Museum’s ‘We Were There’ workshop brings pupils into contact with lived experiences of conflict. Science focuses on light and art responds through Blitz silhouettes, while writing explores argument, viewpoint and narrative. Kindness and Courage run together throughout the unit.

KCA ValueKindness / Courage
Initial experienceWW2: Evacuation Day
Learning presentationWhole School Exhibition on The StreetAuthentic audience: KCA Community
Trip / visit / workshopImperial War Museum – ‘We Were There’ workshop

Writing outcomes

Balanced argument; postcards from different points of view; evacuee narrative

Curriculum focus

History: World War 2 · Science: light · Art: WW2 Blitz silhouette

Summer 1 Big Question

How can ordinary people make an extraordinary difference?

Learning presentationWhole School Exhibition on The Street
Explore this unit +
The learning journey

The World War 2 enquiry continues as pupils deepen their understanding of how individuals and communities respond in difficult circumstances. Historical learning is developed through reporting, suspense writing and information texts about children’s lives during the war. Science turns to electricity, while music explores Songs of World War 2 and the role music can play in memory, morale and shared identity. Pupils continue to return to Kindness and Courage, considering how everyday choices can have an extraordinary impact.

Summer 2 Big Question

How can I become the person I aspire to be?

Learning presentationOpen Afternoon
Explore this unit +
The learning journey

The final Year 6 unit looks forward. Pupils imagine who they might be in thirty years and reflect on the knowledge, habits and choices that can help them become the people they aspire to be. A European study of France connects with their wider curriculum experiences, while science brings previous learning about light and electricity together in a board-game project. Design technology focuses on an upcycled transition cushion and writing supports reflection and advice. Aspiration becomes a bridge between primary school and what comes next.
