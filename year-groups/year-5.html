Autumn 1 Big Question

How can understanding our planet help us make responsible choices?

Learning presentationOpen Afternoon
Explore this unit +
The learning journey

Year 5 begin by exploring the forces that shape our planet and the choices people make in response. Volcano Day sparks curiosity before pupils investigate North America, volcanoes and earthquakes, alongside scientific work on changes in materials. A visit to the Natural History Museum deepens their understanding, while writing and stop-motion animation give pupils different ways to communicate what they have learned. The unit returns to Integrity by asking how knowledge of our planet can help us make responsible choices.

Autumn 2 Big Question

How did the Anglo-Saxons shape the Britain we know today?

Learning presentationKS1 Performance
Explore this unit +
The learning journey

Pupils step into Anglo-Saxon Britain to investigate how migration, settlement and culture helped shape the country we know today. An immersive Anglo-Saxon Day and work with collections at the British Museum provide rich historical evidence. Science explores living things and habitats, while design and technology brings the period to life through an Anglo-Saxon feast. Across the unit, pupils consider Respect: for evidence, for different communities and for the complex stories that make up Britain’s past.

Learning presentationKS1 PerformanceAuthentic audience: Families
Trip / visit / workshopBritish Museum – Anglo-Saxons

Writing outcomes

Grendel narrative; formal letter to King Hygelac; battle flashback recount; guide on how to kill a monster

Curriculum focus

History: Anglo-Saxons · Science: living things and habitats · DT: Anglo-Saxon feast and food technology

Spring 1 Big Question

What can animals teach us about survival and resilience?

Learning presentationWhole School Exhibition on The Street
Explore this unit +
The learning journey

This unit takes pupils across the world’s climate zones and biomes while exploring what survival really requires. The story of Shackleton provides a powerful thread through geography, science and design technology: pupils investigate forces, encounter the Shackleton boat at Dulwich College and apply their learning by designing a boat for his shipmates. Through biography, narrative and reporting, pupils examine human resilience alongside adaptation in the natural world, making Endurance a lived idea rather than simply a value.

KCA ValueEndurance

Learning presentationWhole School Exhibition on The StreetAuthentic audience: KCA Community
Trip / visit / workshopDulwich College – Shackleton Boat

Writing outcomes

Endurance discovery newspaper report; alternative-ending narrative; Ernest Shackleton biography

Curriculum focus

Geography: climate zones & biomes · Science: forces · DT: build a boat for Shackleton and his shipmates

Spring 2 Big Question

What can the Vikings teach us about kindness and community?

Learning presentationWhole School Exhibition on The Street
Explore this unit +
The learning journey

Viking mythology provides the dramatic starting point for an enquiry into community, belief and life in Viking Britain. Pupils explore historical evidence and stories, take part in Freshwater Theatre’s ‘Thorgil the Viking’ workshop and connect their learning with science through the properties of materials. Printmaking provides an artistic response to the period. The Big Question encourages pupils to look beyond familiar images of Vikings and consider what their communities can teach us about Kindness and belonging.

KCA ValueKindness

Writing outcomes

Farm/longhouse setting narrative; temple diary; non-chronological report on Odin and Valhalla

Summer 1 Big Question

How does exploring the unknown change what we know?

Learning presentationWhole School Exhibition on The Street
Explore this unit +
The learning journey

An immersive Wonder Dome experience launches Year 5 beyond Earth and into an enquiry about exploration, discovery and change. Pupils develop scientific knowledge of Earth and Space while geography considers climate change and sustainability. They apply scientific and design thinking to a space-vehicle project and communicate ideas through speeches, reports, poetry and explanation writing. The unit places Courage at its heart, asking pupils to consider why people explore the unknown and how new knowledge can change our understanding of the world.

KCA ValueCourage
Initial experienceIn-school Wonder Dome Space & Earth workshop
Learning presentationWhole School Exhibition on The StreetAuthentic audience: KCA Community
Trip / visit / workshopScience Dome

Writing outcomes

Climate-change speech/news report; Mars poetry; explanation text linked to the space vehicle

Curriculum focus

Geography: climate change & sustainability · Science: Earth & Space · DT: design and evaluate a space vehicle

Summer 2 Big Question

What can we learn from the Tudors about power, ambition and change?

Learning presentationOpen Afternoon
Explore this unit +
The learning journey

The year concludes in Tudor England, where pupils investigate power, ambition and change. A Tudor Experience Day establishes the historical context before pupils use portraiture as both artistic and historical evidence, including a visit to the Portrait Gallery. Science explores human development and ageing, while writing gives pupils opportunities to argue, inform and reshape narratives. Through the value of Aspiration, pupils consider how ambition can drive change while also examining the consequences of how power is used.

KCA ValueAspiration
Initial experienceTudor Experience Day
Learning presentationOpen AfternoonAuthentic audience: Families
Trip / visit / workshopPortrait Gallery

Writing outcomes

Balanced argument; information writing on the slave trade and abolition; alternative-ending narrative

Curriculum focus

History: Tudors · Science: human development and old age · Art: Tudor portrait drawing
